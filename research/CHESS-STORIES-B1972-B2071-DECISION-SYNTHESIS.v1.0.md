## Source correction — do not flatten the 1942 identity
Edward Winter's [Duplication of Chess Games](https://www.chesshistory.com/winter/extra/duplication.html) gives the detailed Russner–Walcker secondary score headed **H.R. Russner – Carl Walcker**. The author's [Byrne–Fischer notes](https://www.chesshistory.com/winter/extra/byrnefischer.html) attribute an archival correction by Henryk Konaszczuk (from *Goniec Krakowski*, 22 November 1942, p3) as **H.W. Russner – K. Walcker**, and identify earlier erroneous variants Rüssher/Walcicer. The historical record has editorial layers: we have **NOT** independently seen the 1942 original, so do not collapse these name forms into verified primary identities.

## Specific printed score comparison (unexecuted legality)
The 1942 secondary sequence has 11.Be3 Qa5+ 12.Bd2 Qa3 13.Rb1 Nxd4 14.Bb4 Nxf3+ 15.Kf1 Be6 16.Be2 Qxa2. Thus Black's queen appears at **a3** preceding ...Be6 and plays **...Qxa2** afterwards in that modern transcription. The EP01 1956 candidate instead places the black queen on **b6** at T01, records 17...Be6 then **18.Bxb6**, capturing the queen. These are textual distinctions between two secondary move lists, **not** an independently qualified replay or proven exact geometric comparison. A common Be6 motif cannot establish the same full board position.
