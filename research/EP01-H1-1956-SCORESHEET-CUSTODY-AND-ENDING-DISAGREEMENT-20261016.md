**New:** Contemporary **1992 first-person observation of two actual player scoresheets** expressly reports disagreement: Fischer's sheet depicts mate, Byrne's indicates resignation one move earlier. A modern scholarly chess-book excerpt establishes that a widely reproduced "original Fischer scoresheet" is **actually a later handwritten reconstruction**, distinct from a reported true original. These are material provenance distinctions, not merely modern PGN opinions.

## Witness D: firsthand account by Hanon W. Russell, posted 20 Apr 1992
Historical discussion preserved at:
https://groups.google.com/g/rec.games.chess/c/DL0Gd9W_9PY
Russell explicitly states he examined original scoresheets of Fischer **and Donald Byrne**, both then in the Russell Collection, and describes **two distinct endings**: mate on Fischer's sheet, Byrne resigns **one move before that mate** on Byrne's. Russell also reports Fischer's 17th move struck out and rewritten, an original-object paleographic detail worth testing against an authenticated image. He asked Robert ("Bob") Byrne, who attended the game, but he could not remember which was right.

Classification: **first-person historical testimony of manuscript inspection**, publicly posted in 1992; not current physical inspection, verified ownership, adjudicated arbiter record or direct witness of actual chess-room termination. The exact final move/pawn on Byrne's original sheet has NOT been independently read. This witness directly challenges any firm statement that checkmate was demonstrably delivered on-board.

## Witness E: John Donaldson's "Inside the Mind of Bobby Fischer" (book preview 2025)
Publisher-authored book listing: https://www.silmanjamespress.com/shop/chess/inside-the-mind-of-bobby-fischer/
Publisher PDF preview, text indexed; PDF direct image-reader failed:
https://www.silmanjamespress.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/INMIBO-flipbook.pdf

**Critical provenance warning:** Donaldson explains that a famous hand-written game sheet is **NOT the original or carbon**, but a Fischer **reconstruction made shortly after the game** for Jack Collins. Claims in older ChessBase/ChessBase India photo captions presenting a particular sheet as *original* must therefore be qualified until image identity is confirmed. The publisher preview specifically describes distinct surviving manuscripts:
- the **original Fischer scoresheet**, according to Donaldson, **held by David DeLucia** (ownership as reported in this work, not re-verified independently);
- the **later Fischer reconstruction**, described in Lilly Library's Collins manuscript holdings;
- the original game scoresheet's alleged custody chain: arbiter Kmoch -> David Lawson -> Burt Hochberg (circa 1980) -> Hanon Russell -> David DeLucia. Some early transfers are expressly uncertain, whereas Russell-to-DeLucia is described as confirmed by Donaldson.
- Byrne's original scoresheet (examined by Russell in 1992) is **a separate object**; its present custodian/location is NOT established by this research.
- The separate **Fischer carbon copy** reportedly retained in Fischer's pocket per Frank Brady; not conflated with either the original or the later reconstruction.
- Donaldson also documents that the **Fischer/Jack Collins notes appeared in Chess Life 20 Dec 1956 p.6**; confirms the source target already found in publisher-hosted primary PDF.

**Do not collapse FOUR physical/documentary classes:** Fischer true original, Byrne true original, Fischer carbon (reported but not located), Fischer later autograph reconstruction.
