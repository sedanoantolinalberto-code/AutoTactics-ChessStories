## Provenance claims are observations of publisher's text, not independently certified original-object history

Donaldson's publisher preview describes: Hans Kmoch retained tournament originals, copies went to players, later original-score ownership through David Lawson, Burt Hochberg, Hanon Russell and David DeLucia, and explicitly acknowledges **uncertainty in intermediate ownership transfers**. It says the Lilly Library Collins manuscript item is Fischer's later score reconstruction, not the original or his contemporaneous carbon.

This corroborates two **documented claims** previously reported by email:
1. The New In Chess editor-in-chief, in a directly read **22 October 2026** email, identifies the score reproduced in **New In Chess 2024/3** as the original in David DeLucia's collection (provider message `1a162886b3737197`).
2. The official New In Chess issue table of contents independently indexes **page 82, “The Scoresheet”**, as Byrne–Fischer 1956 in Fischer's handwriting: https://www.newinchess.com/new-in-chess-2024-3

**Independence caveat:** corroboration of how *publications describe their photographic objects* is **not proof of independent custody lines** if the authors/publishers relied on the same collector or predecessor image. Do not assert that the exact image in this 2025 preview is pixel-for-pixel the exact image reproduced in New In Chess 2024/3 without separately seeing and comparing the latter.

## 26 October addendum — photograph-specific short anchor comparison (non-blind)

A fresh direct **visual screenshot** of the publisher PDF's **zero-index page 23** shows the handwritten table on the **original-attributed** photographed sheet, distinct from printed chess analysis on p22 and from the **Collins reconstruction on p24**. The image visibly has the game-table rows **17**, **18** and **25** in frame. Provisional non-blind readings at these positions are **black row 17 `B-K3`**, **white row 18 `BxQ`**, and **black row 25 `NxR`**. The same short descriptive move classes were observed in the **separate** 1956 US Chess-supplied *Chess Review* pp374–375 facsimile (printed `17...B-K3!!`, `18 BxQ`, `25...NxR`). This is a **limited, non-blind visual token concordance** between a *publisher-attributed original manuscript photograph* and a contemporaneous printed account.

**Limits:** the photo is reproduced at low enough quality, with partially degraded/occluded pen strokes, that none of the three tokens is being certified as an independent *blind* transcription; both the publisher's own object identification and the printed typeset comparison remain separate evidence layers. No direct raw photo SHA-256, chain of physical custody, SAN square inference, source FEN qualification, 14-ply gap replay, complete 82-ply transcription or end-of-game authentication is established. The original-attributed page **does not visibly present the full physical terminal record**; do not borrow the `Mate` note from the different Collins reconstruction on PDF p24.

The **original-attributed page's heading** also appears to identify the Rosenwald event and **Round 8**, while accompanying modern prose assigns a venue; neither automatically settles independent contested round/venue records. Do not make final video's historical room/venue dependent on this publisher reproduction without source adjudication. The documentary result is **new direct access to a reproduced image and three tentative token matches only**; the project's model M, Policy B, R6, IVV, case approval, rules oracle, render and publication gates remain unchanged.
