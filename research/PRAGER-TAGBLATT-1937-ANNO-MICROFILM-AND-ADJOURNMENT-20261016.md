## Why this is new
The Austrian National Library (ÖNB) gives an exact **1937 microfilm holding identity** for *Prager Tagblatt*, not merely the general title catalogue:

- Title register: https://fraueninbewegung.onb.ac.at/node/4588
- Digital title link: http://anno.onb.ac.at/cgi-content/anno?aid=ptb
- Physical print holding: **ÖNB 394189-D.Neu-Per** for volumes 1877–1934, 1936 and 1938 (incomplete).
- **Microform holding: ÖNB MF 3382**, explicitly including **62.1937** (also 1935, and Jan–Apr 1939).
- Austrian National Library archived newspaper register also lists **Prager Tagblatt 1877–1939**, with **1935–1939** holdings, distinguishing paper and microform. https://webarchiv.onb.ac.at/web/20260315081614/https%3A/search.onb.ac.at/primo-explore/custom/ONB/html/informationsliste_zeitungen.html

This gives a concrete fall-back medium **microfilm 62.1937, MF 3382** for a human/institutional request if the ANNO page viewer remains inaccessible. It does **not** show that Flohr's article actually appears on 9 October in the version held; the Polish JBC publication's source credit remains the sole directly inspected evidence for Flohr/date.

### Further printed-quotation transmission lineage to trace
Georges Bertola's article *Alekhine, la reconquête (1)*, Europe Échecs, retrospectively quotes a player statement which it ascribes to *Revue Suisse d'Echecs* about the opening game and says the first game was **adjourned and concluded the following day**. This is **secondary historical reporting of a quoted primary publication**, not the original Swiss periodical inspected by us:
https://www.europe-echecs.com/art/alekhine-la-reconquete-1-8197.html

This corroborates the *subject* of an adjournment at the level of separate publication references, but does not prove the exact sealed-move mechanics or independence of all accounts. JBC 1937 *Przegląd Szachowy* pp.187–189 visibly reports an adjournment and a sealed 41st White move.

Bertola's article also attributes to Kasparov a modern critique of position around moves **14.Ne4 / 16...bxa6**, which may be useful *editorially* but must remain explicitly a later analysis, not a contemporary Flohr claim.
