# Neutral descriptive-notation handout — reader copy v0.1

**For one independent reader and one assigned historical printed witness only.** This handout contains **no game score, no answers, no modern move transcription and no source links**. Do not consult the internet, modern game databases or another reader while transcribing.

## English descriptive notation cheat sheet

| Piece symbol | Name | Other common form |
|---|---|---|
| K | King | — |
| Q | Queen | — |
| R | Rook | — |
| B | Bishop | — |
| N | Knight | Kt |
| P | Pawn | — |

Printed file names, from White's left to right on a standard board: `QR | QN (QKt) | QB | Q | K | KB | KN (KKt) | KR`. These identify queen-rook, queen-knight, queen-bishop, queen, king, king-bishop, king-knight and king-rook files.

Printed ranks `1–8` count from the **moving player's own side**, not from White's side for all moves. The same square can therefore have two descriptive names. **Do not convert source tokens into modern SAN during the first transcription phase.**

A dash generally marks movement, `x` or `×` a capture, `ch` / `+` check, and `mate` a mating notation or adjacent publication comment. `O-O` and `O-O-O` may denote castling. Exclamation marks, question marks, parentheses and textual notes may be annotations or side variations rather than played moves.

**Important:** in a capture, the printed token may identify the *captured piece*, not a destination square. Do not invent a destination or a missing mover because the printed shorthand looks ambiguous.

## Transcription only, before any move conversion

1. Look only at your **assigned page(s)** and write each printed descriptive token literally in the empty worksheet row for the corresponding move/side.
2. Keep `Kt` and `K` distinct; preserve visible spelling, punctuation and any unclear symbol as a noted ambiguity.
3. Record your confidence and the exact issue/page/column/row locator for each token. If a part is unreadable or missing, enter an explicit uncertainty/missing note; never reconstruct it from a remembered game.
4. Exclude annotated side lines from the played score unless a qualified later reviewer determines otherwise; preserve their location in the notes rather than silently deleting the evidence.
5. Freeze and timestamp the **raw** worksheet and attest that no modern game score or other reader's record was consulted.
6. Only after raw freeze may you begin a separately recorded conversion stage. Do not overwrite the raw tokens. Freeze conversion independently.
7. Send the completed record for independent comparison; do not coordinate with the second reader before both records are frozen.

**Your task is to report what the printed publication says, not what the game was expected to say.** No reader is asked to determine historical authenticity, chess legality, copyright ownership or commercial suitability.
